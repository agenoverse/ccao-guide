### Part 1 — Q8 · Delegation Loop · D6 · Select TWO

Sana, a marketing team lead, uses Claude to generate a monthly performance report. The report includes metrics pulled from the team's dashboard, trend analysis, and strategic recommendations. Sana reviews the report carefully, verifies the key metrics against the dashboard, corrects one number, and presents it to the VP of Marketing.

The VP asks: "This recommendation to increase social media spend by 40% — is that your recommendation or the AI's?"

Which TWO of the following statements are correct?

**A.** The recommendation belongs to Claude — Sana should tell the VP that AI generated it and let the VP decide whether to trust it.

**B.** The recommendation belongs to Sana — once she verified and accepted the report, her name is on it. She should be prepared to explain and defend the reasoning, whether she wrote it or Claude did.

**C.** Sana should have removed the recommendation section entirely since AI shouldn't make strategic suggestions.

**D.** Sana should be transparent that AI assisted in generating the report, AND she should be able to explain the reasoning behind the recommendation — because accountability doesn't transfer to AI, but transparency about the process is also good practice.

> [!NOTE]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct: B and D**
>
> **Why B is correct:** This is the accountability rule from the Delegation Loop. Sana VERIFIED the metrics, CORRECTED a number, and ACCEPTED the result. The moment she presents it, it's hers. "Claude made this recommendation" is not a defense if the recommendation is bad. She's accountable for everything she presents — including recommendations she chose to keep rather than edit or remove.
>
> **Why D is correct:** Transparency about AI assistance is good practice — it's honest communication about the process. But transparency is ADDED TO accountability, not a REPLACEMENT for it. Sana can say "I used AI to help draft this report, and here's why the 40% increase makes sense based on our Q2 data." That's transparent AND accountable.
>
> **Why A is wrong:** "The AI recommended it" shifts accountability to Claude, which is exactly what the Delegation Loop says you cannot do. AI doing the work does not transfer accountability to AI. Sana verified, accepted, and presented — it's hers.
>
> **Why C is wrong:** AI can make useful strategic suggestions — the issue is whether the HUMAN reviewed, verified, and can defend the reasoning.

### Part 2 — Q8 · Concepts 4+5+6 Combined · D2/D3 · Select TWO

Hira is building a market entry analysis for her company's expansion into a new city. She uses Claude with Research mode. Her prompt:

> "Research the retail market in Faisalabad. Find market size, top competitors, average rent for commercial space, consumer spending patterns, and any regulatory requirements. Put it all in a report I can present to the board."

Claude produces a polished 3-page report with all the requested sections, specific figures, and a professional tone. Hira reads through it and thinks it's excellent.

Which TWO steps should Hira take before presenting this to the board?

**A.** Ask Claude: "Is everything in this report accurate?" — if Claude confirms, the report is verified.

**B.** Check each critical figure against its source — open the links (if Claude provided them), or search for the original data independently. Any figure without a traceable source should be marked UNVERIFIED or replaced with data from a known source.

**C.** Have a colleague read the report for grammar and formatting — a second pair of eyes ensures quality.

**D.** Verify that no confidential company data was included in the prompt that shouldn't have been shared with an AI system — and check that the report doesn't contain any figures or claims that could embarrass the company if the source turns out to be wrong.

> [!NOTE]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct: B and D**
>
> **Why B is correct:** Every critical figure needs source tracing: where did Claude get "average commercial rent is PKR X per sq ft"? If there's a link, click it and compare. If there's no source, the figure is unverified — either find a source independently or mark it UNVERIFIED. Plausible-sounding numbers from AI are not board-ready without verification.
>
> **Why D is correct:** Two checks: (1) BACKWARD — did Hira paste any confidential company strategy or internal financials into the prompt? (2) FORWARD — if any figure in the report is wrong and the board makes a decision based on it, can Hira defend where the data came from? Both checks happen before the presentation.
>
> **Why A is wrong:** Claude confirming its own output is circular verification. The same system that might have hallucinated a market size figure will confidently say "yes, this is accurate."
>
> **Why C is wrong:** A perfectly formatted report with a hallucinated market size figure will pass grammar review. The critical checks are data accuracy (B) and data safety (D).

### Part 3 — Q8 · Concepts 7+8+9 Combined · D5/D7 · Select TWO

A department head sets up a Claude Project for quarterly business reviews. Six months in, they notice:

- Claude sometimes makes recommendations that exceed the team's spending authority
- The Project Knowledge still contains last year's strategic priorities
- When Claude produces something unexpected, team members either accept it as-is or start over from scratch — nobody adjusts mid-task

Which TWO changes would have the most impact?

**A.** Upgrade to a more capable model for better business judgment.

**B.** Add explicit Authority boundaries to Project Instructions: "Recommendations must stay within the team's quarterly budget of $X. For any recommendation exceeding this, flag it as 'Requires Director Approval' instead of presenting it as a direct suggestion."

**C.** Update Project Knowledge with current strategic priorities, budget figures, and organizational changes — and set a quarterly review cadence to prevent Knowledge from going stale again.

**D.** Train the team to always start over when Claude produces something unexpected — fresh starts produce better results than corrections.

> [!NOTE]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct: B and C**
>
> **Why B is correct:** The spending authority problem is a missing Authority boundary. Adding "flag anything above $X as requiring Director Approval" creates the boundary pattern — Claude can recommend freely but must flag when it crosses the authority line.
>
> **Why C is correct:** Old Knowledge produces old results. Updating the Knowledge fixes the immediate problem, but the quarterly review cadence prevents it from happening again. Projects aren't set-and-forget — they need maintenance as business context changes.
>
> **Why A is wrong:** A more capable model won't know the team's spending authority or current strategic priorities if that information isn't in the Project. The issues are in Instructions (missing boundaries) and Knowledge (stale data), not model intelligence.
>
> **Why D is wrong:** This is the opposite of Concept 9. Starting over wastes all accumulated context. The six intervention moves exist so you don't have to restart.

### Part 4 — Q18 · Cross-Concept (Full Framework) · D1/D2/D3 · Select TWO

An associate needs to build a quarterly market analysis for their company's expansion planning. They have access to Claude with Research mode. Which TWO practices represent the most effective use of the complete delegation framework?

**A.** Write a detailed Delegation Brief (outcome, context, constraints, authority, deliverable, verification), send it to Claude with Research mode, then read the result and forward it to their manager if it looks good.

**B.** Write a Delegation Brief with a clear deliverable format, include the Two Magic Source Instructions ("show URL or document name for each claim" and "write UNVERIFIED for any figure you couldn't verify"), then run ITCD on the result — tracing critical claims to their sources before presenting.

**C.** Skip the brief and use Research mode with a simple prompt — Research mode's web search capability means the results will be current and accurate.

**D.** Write a Delegation Brief, set Authority boundaries ("research freely, but flag any claim you couldn't verify with a primary source"), apply the Privacy Gate before pasting any company data, and include a Verification line that says exactly what you'll check — then follow through with ITCD when the result comes back.

> [!NOTE]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct: B and D**
>
> **Why B is correct:** Brief (Concept 3) + Two Magic Source Instructions (Concept 4) + ITCD (Concept 10). The source instructions make verification possible by forcing Claude to show its work, making the Trace step of ITCD dramatically easier.
>
> **Why D is correct:** Adds Authority ("flag what you couldn't verify") + Privacy Gate (check before pasting company data) + ITCD verification. This is the full framework: Brief → Privacy → Authority → Deliverable → ITCD.
>
> **Why A is wrong:** "Read the result and forward it if it looks good" is the classic trap — reading is not verifying. The brief is good but the verification step is missing.
>
> **Why C is wrong:** Research mode is a tool, not a process. It doesn't replace the Brief, Source instructions, or ITCD.
