## THE ENTIRE COURSE IN ONE SENTENCE

> **Give AI the job. Watch it work. Check what matters. Then decide.**

Two frameworks hold everything:
1. **The Delegation Loop** - 6 steps (the rhythm)
2. **The Delegation Brief** - 6 questions (the written form)

## CONCEPT 1: ASKING vs GIVING THE JOB

```
ASKING:                              GIVING THE JOB:
"What are some good courses          "Find three free courses for
 for learning AI agents?"             learning AI agents and put them
                                      in a table with provider, language,
                                      time needed, and a link."

  Result: a list                       Result: a table with links
  Next steps: YOURS                    Next steps: CHECKING
  Can you hand it off? NO              Can you hand it off? YES
  Can you verify it? HARD              Can you verify it? YES (open links)
```

**The difference is NOT length.** It's that the job asks for a THING TO EXIST.

**To delegate** = give AI the responsibility for producing the result.

Works on any plan, any model. Depends on what kind of sentence you type, not what you pay.

## CONCEPT 2: OUTCOME, NOT CLICKS

Two ways to hand over the same job:

| Approach | What Happens | Problem |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Recipe** (dictate steps) | AI follows YOUR plan, even where it's weak | Your plan is the LIMIT. If your chosen sites aren't the best, the result is neat but poor. |
| **Outcome** (describe result) | AI chooses the method, you judge the result | AI might find better sources you didn't think of. |

**When steps ARE appropriate:** When a source or order of work is REQUIRED -- that's a constraint (Concept 3), not a recipe.

**When steps are NOT appropriate:** When you're writing them because you're nervous. That feeling belongs at the END (when you check), not the START.

**Formula:** "When this is done, I will have [the thing]."

## CONCEPT 3: THE DELEGATION BRIEF (Core Framework)

Six questions you answer BEFORE AI starts. The work order, not a form.

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                    THE DELEGATION BRIEF                           |
|                                                                  |
|  1. OUTCOME       What should exist when the work is complete?   |
|                                                                  |
|  2. CONTEXT       What AI needs to know that it can't know       |
|                   on its own (including any source you give)      |
|                                                                  |
|  3. CONSTRAINTS   Requirements and limits that apply to the job  |
|                                                                  |
|  4. AUTHORITY *   What AI may decide WITHOUT asking you           |
|                   (the line people SKIP)                         |
|                                                                  |
|  5. DELIVERABLE   The FORM the result takes (table, doc, file)   |
|                   shaped so someone can USE it                   |
|                                                                  |
|  6. VERIFICATION* What you will CHECK before accepting           |
|                   (written BEFORE the work starts)               |
|                   (the other line people SKIP)                   |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
  * = the two lines people skip. These are the most important.
```

**Why it matters:** Every time a result disappoints you, one of these six was unanswered. Now you can say WHICH.

**It's a thinking tool, not a form.** Nobody fills in six boxes for a two-line job. But knowing which question was missing is the diagnostic.

### Worked Example

```
Outcome: three free online courses for learning AI agents, compared,
with one recommended for a complete beginner.

Context: I have never built or used an AI agent, and I do not know
how to program. I can study about five hours a week.

Constraints: only courses that can be started for free, and only
courses that do not expect programming knowledge. Search for current
information rather than answering from what you already know.

Authority: choose the sources yourself. If two courses are equal,
pick the shorter one without asking me. Say clearly if a course is
paid, or if it expects me to know how to program.

Deliverable: a short table with provider, language, time needed,
whether a certificate costs money, and a link. Then a two-sentence
recommendation.

Verification: mark anything you could not confirm as "unverified"
instead of guessing.
```

### How the Brief Maps to AI Fluency's Four Competencies

| Brief Line | Competency |
|-----------|-----------|
| Outcome | Delegation + Description |
| Context | Description |
| Constraints | Description + Diligence |
| Authority | Delegation + Diligence |
| Deliverable | Description |
| Verification | Discernment + Diligence |

## CONCEPT 4: RESEARCH (Current Information)

AI has two ways of knowing things:

| Source | Characteristics | When to Use |
|--------|----------------|------------|
| **Remembering** (training data) | Large, out of date. Fact arrives ALONE, sounds just as sure. | Background knowledge, concepts |
| **Searching** (web) | Current, with sources. Fact should arrive WITH a source you can open. | Prices, dates, links, whether something still exists |

**The rule:** When the job needs what is true TODAY, make AI search, and CHECK that it searched.

**How to verify it searched:**
- A found fact comes WITH a source you can open
- A remembered fact arrives ALONE
- No source = don't assume it looked, regardless of what it says about itself
- A source is NOT a date -- find the date on the PAGE yourself

**Add this line to force transparency:**
```
For each item, say whether you found it by searching just now
or already knew about it, and give the date of the page you used.
```

**Web search vs Deep research mode:**
- Web search = one fact that one search settles
- Deep research = job whose deliverable IS the report (takes minutes, reads many pages)

> **"A citation is not verification. Checking the citation is verification."**

## CONCEPT 5: THE SOURCE

When the job needs something AI can't search for (your syllabus, your office notice, a message thread).

**Two instructions that turn pasted text into a source:**

```
1. "Using only the text below"     (stay inside it)
2. "Quote the sentence you used"   (show your work)
```

Together they make **"not in the source"** a GOOD answer instead of a gap AI fills for you.

**Privacy rules BEFORE pasting:**
- Remove names, phone numbers, account numbers, anything you wouldn't pin to a public notice board
- For sensitive work: use temporary chat (ChatGPT) or incognito chat (Claude)
- Neither makes the paste disappear -- provider still holds a copy for a time

**When a long chat goes stale:** Ask AI to summarize what's been decided + what remains, copy it, open fresh chat, paste as first message. The summary is a source too.

**For short text:** Paste inline.
**For long documents:** Attach the file (plus/paperclip button).

## CONCEPT 6: THE DELIVERABLE

**Answer vs Deliverable:**

| Answer | Deliverable |
|--------|------------|
| Replies to YOU | Shaped for someone to USE, save, hand on |
| Lives in the chat | Can leave the chat (doc, file, spreadsheet) |
| Read once, done | Kept, passed on, acted on |

**Ask for the FORM:** table with named columns, document with sections, spreadsheet with formulas, file to download.

**Critical rule:** Add this line to every brief:
```
Keep every "unverified" mark exactly where it is.
```

**Why:** Deliverables LOSE caveats. An "unverified" mark that survived three chat replies can vanish when poured into a tidy document. Count caveats in chat vs document. If the document has fewer, find what was dropped.

## CONCEPT 7: AUTHORITY (Two Faces)

### Face 1: How much of the METHOD AI decides

**Plan-first** = ask AI to list steps and wait for go-ahead BEFORE starting.

```
Before you start, list the steps you plan to take, in order,
and wait for my go-ahead. Do not begin until I say go.
```

**Why:** The cheapest moment in any job is BEFORE it runs. A plan costs a minute to read. The same mistake found AFTER costs the whole run.

An approved plan is NOT a cage. AI may find a better route. What it may NOT do: cross a line you drew, or change something that matters WITHOUT TELLING YOU. The failure = silent change, not the change itself.

### Face 2: What AI may DO without asking (Boundaries)

| Boundary | Why It Exists |
|----------|--------------|
| Research, but do not purchase | Money that moves is hard to move back |
| Draft, but do not send | Sending creates a promise someone else sees |
| Inspect, but do not delete | Deleting destroys evidence needed to check |
| Choose, but say when you chose | A silent decision can't be reviewed |

**Trust grows with a record.** First run: read the plan AND every step. Fifth clean run: read the plan. This is how General Agents turns these into permission modes for unattended work.

### Four Questions Before AI Touches Anything Real

1. What may it read?
2. What may it do?
3. Do I trust where its information comes from?
4. Am I allowed to give it this access at all?

## CONCEPT 8: THE JOB AGAIN (Repetition)

```
1st time: Run the brief. Check results.
2nd time: Save the brief outside the chat. Run again. Compare.
3rd time: Put what doesn't change WHERE AI READS IT BY ITSELF.
```

### Where to Put Recurring Context

| What It Is | Where It Goes |
|-----------|--------------|
| Sources that don't change | **Project knowledge** (files AI reads in every chat inside the project) |
| Rules that don't change | **Standing instructions** (rules AI follows in every chat inside the project) |
| Today's specific job | The chat message |

**Project** = a space that holds files + standing instructions. Every chat inside it starts already informed.

**Standing instruction** = a rule written once, followed in every chat inside the project. Example: "mark anything you could not confirm as unverified."

**Memory vs Project:** Both assistants keep memory about you (if enabled). Once a month, check BOTH what you stored and what AI remembered. Remove what's no longer true. A wrong line in a Project = a wrong line in EVERY result.

**Scheduling (optional):**
- ChatGPT: scheduled tasks on paid plans (ordinary feature)
- Claude: scheduled tasks on paid plans via Cowork
- Rule: A brief you saved today is what you schedule. But General Agents teaches what must be true before a job runs unattended.

**When what repeats is the METHOD (not just context):** That's a Skill, taught in Skills & Connectors. This course stops at Projects.

## CONCEPT 9: INTERVENE (Six Moves)

When AI drifts, diagnose by SYMPTOM:

```
SYMPTOM                          MOVE                     SIZE
+-------------------------------+------------------------+-----------+
| Answered a different question | STOP: restate outcome  | 1 line    |
| One fact/line is wrong        | CORRECT: name error +  | 1 sentence|
|                               |   what should replace  |           |
| Heading wrong way, not wrong  | REDIRECT: name the     | 1 sentence|
|   yet                         |   direction            |           |
| The job itself was wrong      | CHANGE the requirement | 1 sentence|
|                               |   (say you changed it) |           |
| Can't do it at this level     | ESCALATE: turn on      | 1 action  |
|                               |   thinking / stronger  |           |
|                               |   model                |           |
| Keeps failing after escalate  | TAKE IT BACK: some     | --        |
|                               |   jobs are yours       |           |
+-------------------------------+------------------------+-----------+
```

**The move people MISS: Escalate.** Sometimes "AI failed" means "I used the wrong level." Start fast. When the job needs careful comparison or must be right, move up.

**If free plan has no stronger model:** Make the job SMALLER. One step at a time, each checked.

**After every intervention:**
1. Check the fix IN THE RESULT (not in AI's description of the fix)
2. Write the lesson into the brief as a Constraint or Authority line
3. "A claimed fix that did not happen is the failure this step exists to catch."

**Golden rule:** The SMALLEST message that fixes it is the intervention.

## CONCEPT 10: VERIFY BEFORE YOU ACCEPT (The Method)

"Check AI's work" is advice. This is the METHOD.

### Four Steps Inside Verify

```
IDENTIFY ──> TRACE ──> CHALLENGE ──> DECIDE
   |            |          |            |
Which claims  Open the   Ask how it   One of four
matter enough source,    could be     decisions
to check?     find the   wrong
              sentence,
              check the
              date
```

### Step 1: IDENTIFY (What to Check)

Check where an error would COST you:
- Prices, dates, figures, calculations
- Quotations (exact words)
- Legal or safety claims
- The recommendation you'll act on
- Anything hard to reverse

### Step 2: TRACE

- Open the source
- Find the SENTENCE that supports the claim
- Check the DATE on the page
- Ask: is this source good enough? (provider's own page > blog about provider)

### Step 3: CHALLENGE

- Could this have changed since the page was written?
- Is AI repeating a secondary source?
- Is an assumption being stated as a fact?
- When two credible sources disagree: primary usually wins, unless it's older

### Step 4: DECIDE (Four Exits)

```
+----------+  +----------+  +-------------+  +----------+
|  ACCEPT  |  | CORRECT  |  | INVESTIGATE |  |  REJECT  |
| Evidence |  | Specific |  | Evidence is |  | Not      |
| is enough|  | error,   |  | incomplete  |  | reliable |
|          |  | fixable  |  | or contra-  |  | enough   |
| Use the  |  | (= Inter-|  | dictory     |  | to use   |
| result   |  |  vene)   |  | (trace      |  |          |
|          |  |          |  |  again)     |  | Take job |
|          |  |          |  |             |  | back     |
+----------+  +----------+  +-------------+  +----------+
```

> **AI doing the work does NOT transfer accountability to AI. The name on the result is yours.**

## CONCEPT 11: SAME JOB, DIFFERENT AI

**The test of a brief:** It works somewhere else without a rewrite.

**Why it works:** The brief describes the JOB, not the TOOL. Nothing in the six lines names a button. Buttons differ between assistants and change every few months.

**What to record (NOT a ranking):**
- Did it finish the job?
- How many messages needed?
- Did it honor the Authority line?
- Did it mark anything unverified?
- How did the deliverable compare?

**Critical distinction:** Two runs on one day ≠ a ranking of two assistants. Models change too fast.

> **The job belongs to you. The model is replaceable.**

## THE "NEED FIRST, THEN TOOL" MAP

The rule: a control is learned when a job NEEDS it, not before.

| The Job Needs | So You | Concept |
|--------------|--------|---------|
| Current information | Ask for it, turn on web search | 4 |
| A source only you have | Paste it or attach file | 5 |
| Something private in the job | Remove private parts, use temp/incognito chat | 5 |
| A finished thing, not an answer | Ask for the document/table/file itself | 6 |
| Room to choose its own method | Say what it may decide alone + what it must ask | 7 |
| The same context every time | Put sources in Project as knowledge, rules as instructions | 8 |
| The same job on a clock | Scheduled task (where plan has one) | 8 |
| More capability than it showed | Turn on thinking / stronger model | 9 |
| A place for the result / source in another app | Connector | 7 + Skills course |

## EXAM POWER RULES

1. **Asking = answer (work stays with you). Job = result (you just check).**
2. **Describe the OUTCOME, not the steps.** Steps limit AI to the quality of YOUR plan.
3. **Six lines: Outcome, Context, Constraints, Authority, Deliverable, Verification.** Authority + Verification are the two people skip.
4. **A citation is not verification. CHECKING the citation is verification.**
5. **"Using only the text below" + "quote the sentence you used"** = two instructions that make pasted text a real source.
6. **Deliverable ≠ answer.** A deliverable can be handed on, saved, acted on.
7. **Plan-first = cheapest moment to fix a job** (before it runs).
8. **Authority has two faces:** what AI may DECIDE (method) + what AI may DO (actions/permissions).
9. **Six intervention moves:** Stop, Correct, Redirect, Change requirement, Escalate, Take it back. Escalate is the one people miss.
10. **Verify = Identify, Trace, Challenge, Decide.** Four exits: Accept, Correct, Investigate, Reject.
11. **AI doing the work does NOT transfer accountability.** Your name, your responsibility.
12. **A good brief runs in a second AI without rewriting.** It describes the job, not the tool.
13. **Third time you paste the same context = put it in a Project.**
14. **The smallest message that fixes the drift IS the intervention.**
15. **Need first, then tool.** Every button is learned when a job asks for it, not before.

## QUICK SELF-TEST (10 Questions)

**Q1:** What's the difference between asking AI and giving AI a job?
> Asking gets an answer and keeps the next steps with you. A job asks for a thing to exist and leaves you the checking.

**Q2:** Name the 6 steps of the Delegation Loop. Which stay yours?
> Define, Delegate, Observe, Intervene, Verify, Accept. Both ENDS stay yours: Define + Delegate at the start, Verify + Accept at the end.

**Q3:** Why does describing the outcome beat dictating the steps?
> A recipe limits the result to the quality of YOUR plan. An outcome lets AI choose the method and lets you judge the result against what you asked for.

**Q4:** Name the 6 lines of the Delegation Brief. Which two do people skip?
> Outcome, Context, Constraints, Authority, Deliverable, Verification. People skip **Authority** and **Verification**.

**Q5:** How can you tell whether AI searched or answered from memory?
> A searched fact should arrive WITH a source you can open. A remembered fact arrives alone and sounds just as sure. No source = don't assume it looked.

**Q6:** What two instructions turn pasted text into a source?
> "Using only the text below" + "quote the sentence you used." Together they make "not in the source" a good answer.

**Q7:** What's the cheapest moment to fix a job, and how do you get there?
> BEFORE the run. Ask for the plan and wait for go-ahead (plan-first), then change one step.

**Q8:** Third time you paste the same background -- where does it go?
> Into a **Project**: sources as knowledge, rules as standing instructions. The chat carries only today's specific job.

**Q9:** Name the 6 intervention moves. Which one do people miss?
> Stop, Correct, Redirect, Change the requirement, Escalate, Take it back. **Escalate** is the one people miss ("AI failed" sometimes means "I used the wrong level").

**Q10:** What are the 4 steps of verification and the 4 decisions it ends in?
> Steps: **Identify, Trace, Challenge, Decide.** Decisions: **Accept, Correct, Investigate, Reject.**

## PRACTICAL CHEAT SHEET: Writing a Brief in 2 Minutes

```
Step 1: "When this is done, I will have ___"          --> OUTCOME
Step 2: "What does AI not know?"                       --> CONTEXT
Step 3: "What limits matter?"                          --> CONSTRAINTS
Step 4: "What may it decide? What must it ask about?"  --> AUTHORITY
Step 5: "What form should the result take?"            --> DELIVERABLE
Step 6: "What will I check before trusting this?"      --> VERIFICATION

Then add:
  "Mark anything you could not confirm as 'unverified'."
  "If evidence is missing, say so and stop rather than inventing it."
```
