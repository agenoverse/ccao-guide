# Business & Workplace Concepts for CCAO-F

> **Why this guide exists:** The CCAO-F exam puts every question inside a workplace scenario. You don't need to BE a financial analyst or HR manager. You need to UNDERSTAND what the scenario is describing so you can make the right judgment call about AI use.
>
> **How to use:** Read this once. Then when you practice exam questions and hit a word you don't know, come back here. Use the mini-scenarios at the end to test yourself.

---

# PART 1: THE EXAM'S WORLD

## What Kinds of Scenarios Will You See?

The exam is designed for people working in these roles:
- **Operations** (running a business day-to-day)
- **Marketing** (promoting products/services)
- **Project management** (organizing work and teams)
- **Education** (teaching, training)
- **Communications** (writing for companies)
- **HR / Human Resources** (hiring, managing employees)
- **Consulting** (advising other companies)
- **General knowledge work** (office work that involves thinking, writing, analyzing)

**Every question follows this pattern:**
> A person in one of these roles has a task. They want to use Claude. Something about the situation requires a judgment call. What should they do?

The business details are just the SETTING. The answer is always about:
1. Should you verify this output?
2. Is this data safe to upload?
3. Is this the right tool/model?
4. Does a human need to review?
5. What's the right workflow?

# PART 2: BUSINESS VOCABULARY YOU MUST KNOW

## A. The Organization (Company Structure)

### Who Is Who?

| Person/Role | What They Do | Why They Appear in Exam |
|-------------|-------------|------------------------|
| **Executive / C-suite** | Top leaders (CEO, CFO, CTO). Make big decisions. | They ask for summaries and reports. AI output going to them must be accurate and concise. |
| **Manager** | Leads a team. Responsible for team's work. | They approve things. They are often the "human gate" in exam answers. |
| **Stakeholder** | Anyone affected by or interested in a project. Could be a boss, client, team member, or investor. | The exam asks how to communicate AI's value and limits to stakeholders. |
| **Compliance team** | Makes sure the company follows laws and rules. | They review anything related to regulations. AI output for them MUST be verified. |
| **Legal team / Legal counsel** | Company lawyers. | They handle contracts, lawsuits, regulations. AI cannot replace their judgment. |
| **HR / Human Resources** | Hires people, manages employees, handles benefits. | Exam loves HR scenarios because they involve personal data + bias risk. |
| **Finance team / CFO** | Manages money, budgets, reports. | Financial numbers from AI ALWAYS need human verification. |
| **IT / Technical team** | Manages technology and systems. | They set up tools, connectors, security. |
| **Consultant** | Outside expert hired to advise. | Some exam takers ARE consultants. Scenarios may say "you are advising a client." |
| **Client / Customer** | Person or company that pays for your service. | Client-facing content = higher stakes = needs human review. |
| **Vendor** | Company that sells products/services to your company. | Appears in comparison/evaluation scenarios. |

### Key Concept: Internal vs. External

This distinction appears in MANY exam questions:

| | Internal | External |
|---|---------|----------|
| **Who sees it?** | Only people inside your company | People outside: clients, customers, public |
| **Stakes** | Lower (you can fix mistakes easily) | Higher (mistakes damage reputation, trust, money) |
| **Human review needed?** | Sometimes (depends on content) | Almost always yes |
| **Examples** | Team meeting notes, brainstorm ideas, internal draft | Client proposal, marketing ad, investor report, press release |

**Exam rule:** If output goes EXTERNAL, it almost always needs human review before sending.

## B. Business Activities & Processes

### Common Workflows the Exam Uses

**1. Client Onboarding**
- = The process of setting up a new client/customer
- Steps: collect info > verify identity > set up account > welcome them
- AI use: draft welcome emails, organize documents
- Risk: contains personal data (names, addresses, payment info)

**2. Quarterly Review / Quarterly Report**
- = Every 3 months, companies report how things are going
- Contains: financial numbers, performance data, goals, plans
- AI use: summarize data, create charts, draft report
- Risk: financial figures MUST be verified against source data

**3. Content Publishing / Content Pipeline**
- = Creating and releasing blog posts, social media, marketing materials
- Steps: ideate > write > edit > approve > publish
- AI use: draft content, brainstorm ideas, edit for tone
- Risk: external-facing = needs human review for accuracy and brand

**4. Hiring / Recruitment Process**
- = Finding and choosing new employees
- Steps: post job > screen resumes > interview > decide > offer
- AI use: screen resumes, draft job descriptions, summarize candidates
- Risk: MAJOR bias risk. Discrimination laws apply. Always needs human review. Never let AI make final hiring/firing decisions alone.

**5. Expense Approval / Procurement**
- = Approving purchases or spending company money
- Steps: request > review > approve > pay
- AI use: categorize expenses, flag unusual amounts, draft summaries
- Risk: financial data, approval is irreversible once payment goes out

**6. Customer Support / Complaint Handling**
- = Responding to customer problems
- Steps: receive complaint > investigate > respond > follow up
- AI use: draft responses, categorize issues, suggest solutions
- Risk: company is accountable for what it tells customers about their money/account

**7. Contract Review**
- = Reading legal agreements to check terms
- Steps: receive contract > identify key terms > flag risks > negotiate > sign
- AI use: extract clauses, compare against standard terms, flag departures
- Risk: legal consequences. Lawyer MUST review. AI can help but never decides.

**8. Report Generation / Dashboard**
- = Creating visual/written summaries of data
- AI use: analyze data, create summaries, suggest visualizations
- Risk: numbers must be verified. Charts can mislead if data is wrong.

**9. Meeting Minutes / Standup Notes**
- = Written record of what was discussed in a meeting
- AI use: summarize conversation, organize action items
- Risk: LOW for internal. Higher if shared externally or contains decisions.

**10. Compliance Audit**
- = Checking that the company follows rules and regulations
- AI use: summarize regulations, compare practices against requirements
- Risk: HIGH. Compliance content goes to regulators or legal. Always verify.

## C. Financial Terms (Just What You Need)

You will NOT be tested on accounting or finance. But scenarios mention these terms:

### Money & Measurements

| Term | Simple Meaning | Example in Exam Context |
|------|---------------|------------------------|
| **Revenue** | Money coming IN from selling things | "Claude generated a report showing Q3 revenue of $4.2M" |
| **Profit** | Money left AFTER paying all costs | Revenue minus costs = profit |
| **Budget** | A plan for how to spend money | "The team has a limited AI budget" = think about cost when choosing models |
| **ROI (Return on Investment)** | "Was the money we spent worth it?" | "Calculate the ROI of implementing Claude" |
| **Cost-benefit analysis** | Comparing what something costs vs. what you gain | Used when deciding if AI is worth implementing |
| **Quarter (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4)** | Business year split into 4 parts of 3 months each | Q1 = Jan-Mar, Q2 = Apr-Jun, Q3 = Jul-Sep, Q4 = Oct-Dec |
| **Fiscal year** | Company's financial year (may not start in January) | "End of fiscal year" = deadline pressure |
| **Overhead** | Ongoing costs of running a business (not directly making product) | Office rent, salaries, software subscriptions |
| **Forecast / Projection** | Predicting future numbers | "Claude created revenue projections" = MUST verify, these are estimates |
| **Trend analysis** | Looking at data over time to find patterns | "Analyze spending trends" = common AI task |
| **Accounts / Account numbers** | Records of money or customer relationships | Always sensitive data when paired with names |
| **Invoice** | Bill sent to a customer for payment | Contains financial data |
| **Reconciliation** | Checking that two sets of records match | "Reconcile bank statements" = matching your records to the bank's |

### The Only Financial Rule for the Exam

> **If Claude produces any specific number (dollar amount, percentage, statistic), it MUST be verified against the original source data before anyone acts on it.**

That's it. You don't need to know how to calculate these things. You need to know that AI-generated numbers are not automatically trustworthy.

## D. Legal & Compliance Terms

These appear frequently because governance (Domain 6) is 15% of the exam.

| Term | Simple Meaning | Why It Matters for the Exam |
|------|---------------|---------------------------|
| **Regulation** | Official rule made by government | AI output about regulations must be verified against the actual law |
| **Compliance** | Following the rules | A "compliance team" reviews whether the company is following rules |
| **Policy** | Company's own rules (not law, but company rules) | "Organizational AI policy" = your company's rules about using AI |
| **PII (Personally Identifiable Information)** | Any data that can identify a specific person: name, email, phone, SSN, address | MUST be anonymized or removed before uploading to AI (usually) |
| **GDPR** | European privacy law (very strict) | Protects personal data of European people |
| **HIPAA** | US health data privacy law | Medical records are heavily regulated |
| **FERPA** | US student education privacy law | Student records need special protection |
| **COPPA** | US children's privacy law | Extra protection for data of children under 13 |
| **Anonymize** | Remove all identifying info so no one can figure out who the data is about | "Anonymize before uploading" = the correct exam answer in most data scenarios |
| **Pseudonymize** | Replace real names with fake labels ("Customer 17") but keep a mapping somewhere | Better than nothing, but someone can still trace back. Keeps its data tier. |
| **Redact** | Black out or remove specific sensitive parts | Like anonymizing but for specific fields |
| **Audit trail** | Record of who did what and when | Important for accountability |
| **Data breach** | When sensitive data gets exposed to unauthorized people | What you're trying to prevent |
| **Consent** | Permission from the person whose data it is | "Did the client consent to their data being processed by AI?" |
| **Liability** | Legal responsibility if something goes wrong | "Who is liable?" = always the human/organization, never the AI |
| **Privilege / Privileged** | Legally protected communications (like lawyer-client) | NEVER upload privileged communications to AI |
| **NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)** | Legal promise to keep information secret | Some data can't be shared because of an NDA |
| **IP (Intellectual Property)** | Things a company owns: inventions, brand names, trade secrets | Be careful uploading others' IP to AI |
| **Due diligence** | Doing proper research/checking before a decision | Verifying AI output IS due diligence |

### The Only Legal Rule for the Exam

> **You are not a lawyer on this exam. When a scenario involves legal content (contracts, regulations, compliance reports), the correct answer always includes "have legal/compliance review it." Never let AI be the final authority on legal matters.**

| Term | Simple Meaning | Example |
|------|---------------|---------|
| **Pilot program / Pilot** | A small test of something before rolling it out fully | "Run a pilot with one team before company-wide adoption" |
| **Proof of concept (PoC)** | A small demo to prove an idea can work | "Build a PoC to show the executive team" |
| **Rollout** | The process of making something available to everyone | "Phase 1 rollout to marketing, Phase 2 to all teams" |

| **SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)** | Written instructions for how to do a routine task | AI can help create or follow SOPs |

## J. Process Improvement Terms

The exam asks about "process reimagination" and "identifying opportunities for AI." You need these terms:

| Term | Simple Meaning | Example |
|------|---------------|---------|
| **Bottleneck** | The slowest step that slows everything else down | "Report approval is the bottleneck — everything waits for the manager" |
| **Efficiency** | Doing things faster/cheaper without losing quality | "AI improved efficiency by 40%" |
| **Automation** | Making something happen without human effort | "Automate the data entry step" |
| **Automation candidate** | A task that COULD be automated | Repetitive, rule-based, low-stakes tasks are good candidates |
| **Human-in-the-loop** | A human checks/approves at some point in the process | KEY exam concept: the human checkpoint |
| **Friction** | A small repeated cost or annoyance that slows work down | "Copying data between systems is friction" |
| **Workflow** | The sequence of steps to complete a piece of work | "Map the existing workflow before adding AI" |
| **Process optimization** | Making a process work better | "Optimize the content review process with AI" |
| **Throughput** | How much work gets done in a time period | "AI increased throughput from 10 to 50 reports per day" |
| **Turnaround time** | How long it takes from start to finish | "Reduce turnaround time from 3 days to 4 hours" |

# PART 3: THE INDUSTRIES (Scenarios You'll See)

The exam doesn't test industry expertise. It uses these industries as BACKDROPS:

## Healthcare
- **What shows up:** Patient records, medical data, HIPAA
- **Exam rule:** Medical data = RED tier. Never upload patient identifiers. AI cannot diagnose or prescribe. Doctor/clinician always reviews.

## Finance / Banking
- **What shows up:** Account numbers, transactions, financial reports, regulatory requirements
- **Exam rule:** Financial data with PII = RED tier. Financial numbers = always verify. Client financial data needs consent check.

## Education
- **What shows up:** Student records, grades, essays, parent communication
- **Exam rule:** Student data = protected by FERPA/COPPA. Teacher must review AI feedback before sending to parents. Minors = extra protection.

## Legal
- **What shows up:** Contracts, regulations, court cases, compliance
- **Exam rule:** Legal content = always needs lawyer review. AI can help draft but never decides. Privileged communications = never upload.

## Government / Public Sector
- **What shows up:** Public records, policy documents, citizen data
- **Exam rule:** Citizen data = regulated. Policy decisions affect many people = high stakes.

## Retail / E-commerce
- **What shows up:** Customer data, purchase history, product descriptions, reviews
- **Exam rule:** Customer PII = protected. Product descriptions = OK for AI. Customer communications = review before sending.

# PART 4: CONNECTING BUSINESS CONCEPTS TO EXAM ANSWERS

## The Translation Table

This is the most important section. When you see a business term in a question, HERE is what it means for your answer:

| When the scenario says... | Think... | The answer probably involves... |
|--------------------------|----------|-------------------------------|
| "client-facing" or "customer-facing" | HIGH STAKES, external | Human review before sending |
| "revenue projections" or "financial figures" | NUMBERS = verify | Check against source data |
| "compliance team" or "regulatory" | LEGAL domain | Verify against actual regulation text |
| "employee data" or "salary" or "performance review" | SENSITIVE personal data | Anonymize or protect, human reviews |
| "internal brainstorm" or "rough draft" | LOW STAKES, internal | OK with less review |
| "press release" or "public announcement" | EXTERNAL + represents company | Human review essential |
| "contract" or "legal agreement" | LEGAL = can't undo if wrong | Lawyer must review |
| "patient" or "medical" | HEALTH DATA = highest protection | Never upload identifiers, doctor reviews |
| "student" or "minor" or "child" | EXTRA protections (FERPA/COPPA) | Consent checks, teacher reviews |
| "upload the spreadsheet" | DATA going into AI | Check: does it have PII? Anonymize first? |
| "send it to the client" | OUTPUT going external | Human review before sending |
| "automate" or "automatically" | Removing human from loop | Is there still a human gate? |
| "cost" or "budget" | RESOURCE constraint | Match model to task (don't overspend) |
| "high volume" or "many requests" | EFFICIENCY need | Use faster/cheaper model (Haiku) |
| "complex analysis" or "deep reasoning" | QUALITY need | Use more capable model (Opus) |
| "policy restricts" or "org policy says" | COMPANY RULES | Follow the policy. Don't ignore it. |
| "confident summary" or "specific citation" | HALLUCINATION risk | Verify against the actual source |

# PART 5: PRACTICE SCENARIOS

Read each scenario. Identify the business concepts. Then make the judgment call.

---

### Scenario 1: The Quarterly Report

> A marketing manager asks Claude to analyze last quarter's campaign performance data and generate a report with ROI figures for the executive team. The data includes customer acquisition costs, conversion rates, and revenue per channel.

**Business concepts to spot:**
- "Quarterly" = every 3 months, standard business reporting cycle
- "Campaign performance" = how well the marketing efforts worked
- "ROI" = return on investment, "did the money we spent bring in more money?"
- "Customer acquisition cost" = how much money it costs to get one new customer
- "Conversion rate" = percentage of people who did the desired action (like buying)
- "Revenue per channel" = money earned from each source (social media, email, ads, etc.)
- "Executive team" = top leaders who make decisions based on this report

**What matters for the exam answer:**
- This has FINANCIAL FIGURES (ROI, costs, revenue) = must verify against source data
- Going to EXECUTIVES = high stakes, must be accurate
- The DATA itself (campaign metrics) = probably Yellow tier (internal business data)
- Does it have PII? Campaign performance data usually doesn't have individual customer names

**Likely correct answer pattern:** Use Claude to draft the analysis, but verify all financial figures against the source data before presenting to executives.

---

### Scenario 2: The Resume Screener

> An HR coordinator wants to use Claude to read through 200 job applications and create a shortlist of the top 20 candidates. The applications contain names, addresses, work history, and education details.

**Business concepts to spot:**
- "HR coordinator" = person who handles hiring tasks
- "Job applications" = documents people submit to get a job
- "Shortlist" = a smaller list of the best candidates from a big list
- "Work history" = where the person worked before
- Contains: names, addresses = PII (personally identifiable information)

**What matters for the exam answer:**
- DATA = RED (names + addresses + employment history = PII)
- The DISCRIMINATOR: does AI need the names to screen applications? NO. It can evaluate skills and experience without names.
- BIAS RISK: AI screening can discriminate against certain groups
- This is a DECISION ABOUT PEOPLE = high stakes, needs heavy human review
- A human must review BOTH who's included AND who's excluded (adverse impact check)

**Likely correct answer pattern:** Anonymize the applications (remove names, addresses), let Claude help organize/categorize, but a human HR professional must review the shortlist AND the rejected candidates for bias before any decisions are made.

---

### Scenario 3: The Contract Clause

> A project manager receives a new vendor contract and asks Claude to compare it against the company's standard terms. Claude flags three clauses that differ from the standard and provides a confident explanation of the legal implications.

**Business concepts to spot:**
- "Vendor contract" = legal agreement with a company selling you something
- "Standard terms" = the normal conditions your company usually agrees to
- "Clauses" = individual sections/paragraphs in a contract (each covers one topic)
- "Legal implications" = what this means legally, what could happen

**What matters for the exam answer:**
- This is LEGAL content = lawyer/legal team must review
- Claude's "confident explanation of legal implications" = HALLUCINATION RISK (Claude can fabricate legal analysis)
- The PM is not qualified to judge legal accuracy
- The contract is EXTERNAL (with a vendor) = consequences if wrong

**Likely correct answer pattern:** Claude's flagging of different clauses is helpful as a first pass, but the legal implications must be reviewed by the legal team before any action. Do not rely on Claude's legal interpretation.

---

### Scenario 4: The Data Upload

> A finance analyst wants to upload a spreadsheet containing customer names, account numbers, and transaction amounts to Claude for trend analysis. Company policy restricts sharing regulated personal data with AI tools.

**Business concepts to spot:**
- "Finance analyst" = person who analyzes financial data
- "Customer names + account numbers" = PII = regulated personal data
- "Transaction amounts" = how much money was spent/received
- "Trend analysis" = looking at patterns over time
- "Company policy restricts" = organizational rule you must follow

**What matters for the exam answer:**
- This is EXACTLY like Sample Question 3 from the official exam guide
- DATA = RED (customer names + account numbers = PII + financial data)
- The DISCRIMINATOR: does trend analysis need individual customer names/account numbers? NO. Only the pattern matters.
- POLICY says don't share regulated personal data
- Answer is NOT "skip the analysis" (that abandons the task)
- Answer is NOT "upload as-is" (violates policy)
- Answer is NOT "upload but tell Claude not to retain" (doesn't satisfy policy)

**Correct answer:** Remove/anonymize the personal identifiers (names, account numbers) before uploading, then proceed with the trend analysis. The analysis continues, the policy is followed.

---

### Scenario 5: The Stakeholder Presentation

> A consultant is preparing a presentation for a client's board of directors about implementing AI in their customer service workflow. The consultant used Claude to draft the presentation.

**Business concepts to spot:**
- "Consultant" = outside expert advising a company
- "Board of directors" = the highest decision-making group in a company (even above CEO)
- "Implementing AI" = putting AI into actual use in the business
- "Customer service workflow" = the process for helping customers with problems

**What matters for the exam answer:**
- Going to BOARD OF DIRECTORS = highest stakes audience
- EXTERNAL (client's board, not your own company)
- The presentation must include: what AI does, what humans still do, where the human gates are
- Every claim about AI capabilities must be accurate (no hallucinated benefits)
- Must mention limitations, not just benefits (responsible communication)

**Likely correct answer pattern:** Review every claim for accuracy, ensure the presentation mentions both capabilities AND limitations, include clear human checkpoints in the proposed workflow, and have the consultant (human) personally verify all facts before presenting.

---

### Scenario 6: The Incident

> An employee accidentally uploaded a spreadsheet with customer Social Security numbers to Claude while doing a routine analysis. The employee realizes the mistake immediately.

**Business concepts to spot:**
- "Social Security numbers (SSN)" = the most sensitive personal identifier in the US (9-digit number assigned to every person)
- "Routine analysis" = regular, everyday work
- "Realizes the mistake immediately" = they know it happened right away

**What matters for the exam answer:**
- SSN = RED tier, absolute highest sensitivity
- This is a DATA INCIDENT
- The Incident First Hour framework: CONTAIN > ASSESS > NOTIFY > DOCUMENT
- Don't panic. Don't ignore it. Follow the process.

**Likely correct answer pattern:** Stop the process (contain), assess what data was exposed, notify the appropriate people (manager, security team, possibly legal), and document what happened. Don't try to hide it.

---

### Scenario 7: The Consultant's Recommendation

> You are a consultant advising a retail client on adopting AI for their customer service workflow. The client's team currently handles 500 support tickets per day. You've used Claude to draft an implementation plan showing projected cost savings and efficiency gains. The client's CEO asks you to present this at the next board meeting.

**Business concepts to spot:**
- "Consultant advising a client" = YOU are the outside expert. You carry credibility.
- "Retail client" = a company that sells products to consumers (a shop, online store)
- "Support tickets" = customer requests/complaints in a system (each one is a "ticket")
- "500 per day" = high volume = efficiency matters
- "Implementation plan" = step-by-step plan for putting something into action
- "Projected cost savings" = estimated future money saved (it's a PREDICTION, not a fact)
- "Efficiency gains" = doing things faster/better
- "CEO" = Chief Executive Officer, the top leader
- "Board meeting" = meeting of the board of directors, the highest decision-making group

**What matters for the exam answer:**
- You (consultant) used Claude to draft the plan = the projections are AI-generated estimates
- "Projected cost savings" = FINANCIAL FIGURES = must verify against real data
- Going to CEO + BOARD = highest stakes audience
- You must include BOTH benefits AND limitations of AI
- The plan should show WHERE humans stay in the loop
- You personally (as consultant) are ACCOUNTABLE for what you present

**Likely correct answer pattern:** Verify all projected numbers against real data, ensure the plan includes human checkpoints in the workflow, mention AI's limitations alongside benefits, and review everything personally before presenting. Don't present AI-generated projections as guaranteed facts.

### Scenario 8: The Troubleshooting Scenario

> A team has been using a Claude Project to generate weekly customer satisfaction reports. The reports were accurate for the first two months, but this week's report contains outdated product names and references discontinued services. The team lead wants to know what went wrong.

**Business concepts to spot:**
- "Claude Project" = a saved workspace with instructions and uploaded knowledge files
- "Customer satisfaction reports" = documents measuring how happy customers are
- "Accurate for first two months" = it was working, then stopped working
- "Outdated product names" = old names that the company no longer uses
- "Discontinued services" = services the company stopped offering
- "Team lead" = person managing the team

**What matters for the exam answer:**
- This is a TROUBLESHOOTING scenario (D7 = 10% of exam)
- The problem: output was good, then got WORSE over time
- This pattern = STALE CONFIGURATION (knowledge files haven't been updated)
- The product names changed, the services were discontinued, but the Project's knowledge files still have the old information
- Use the diagnostic sequence: prompt > session > feature/model > config > task
- Here, the issue is CONFIG (#4) — stale knowledge files

**Likely correct answer pattern:** The Project's uploaded knowledge files contain outdated product and service information. Update the knowledge files with current product names and service offerings. Set a maintenance schedule to keep knowledge files current.

# PART 7: THE DECISION SHORTCUT

When you read any exam question, do this in order:

```
1. WHO is in the scenario?    (their role tells you the stakes)
2. WHAT data is involved?     (sensitive? personal? financial? legal?)  
3. WHERE does the output go?  (internal or external?)
4. WHAT could go wrong?       (what's the worst case if AI is wrong?)
5. NOW pick the answer        (using the elimination strategy)
```

### Quick Decision Rules

| If the scenario has... | The answer almost certainly includes... |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Any financial numbers | "Verify against source data" |
| Any legal/compliance content | "Have legal/compliance review it" |
| Any personal data (names, IDs) | "Anonymize before uploading" |
| External audience (clients, public) | "Human reviews before sending" |
| Medical/health information | "Never upload identifiers, doctor reviews" |
| "Send as-is" as an option | That option is probably WRONG |
| "Skip the analysis" as an option | That option is probably WRONG |

# PART 8: 15-MINUTE SELF-TEST

Answer these without looking up. Check your answers below.

**Q1:** What does "ROI" stand for, and why would you verify Claude's ROI calculation?

**Q2:** A scenario mentions "PII." What does that stand for and give 3 examples.

**Q3:** What's the difference between "anonymized" and "pseudonymized" data?

**Q4:** A "compliance team" is reviewing your AI-generated report. What is their job?

**Q5:** A scenario says output is "client-facing." Does this raise or lower the stakes? Why?

**Q6:** A question mentions "adverse impact" in an HR context. What does this mean?

**Q7:** What does "SLA" mean and why might it appear in an exam question?

**Q8:** The phrase "rubber stamp" appears in a workflow description. Is this good or bad? Why?

**Q9:** A "vendor contract" needs review. Can Claude's analysis be the final check? Why or why not?

**Q10:** A scenario says "the data contains SSNs." What tier is this and what should you do?

---

### Answers

**A1:** Return on Investment. Verify because AI can fabricate convincing-looking financial figures. Numbers must always be checked against source data.

**A2:** Personally Identifiable Information. Examples: names, email addresses, phone numbers, Social Security numbers, home addresses, account numbers, date of birth.

**A3:** Anonymized = no one can identify the person from the data. Pseudonymized = names are replaced with labels ("Customer 17") but a mapping list exists that could re-identify people. Pseudonymized data keeps its data sensitivity tier.

**A4:** Their job is to make sure the company follows laws, regulations, and internal policies. Content going to them must be verified against actual regulation text.

**A5:** RAISES the stakes. Client-facing means external people see it. Mistakes damage trust, reputation, and business relationships. Always needs human review.

**A6:** Adverse impact = a process (like AI screening) unfairly affects one group more than others. Example: AI rejects 80% of female applicants but only 30% of male applicants. This is illegal discrimination and a major red flag.

**A7:** Service Level Agreement = a promise about service quality. In an exam question, it might test whether AI can help monitor/meet SLAs, or whether AI-generated responses meet SLA standards.

**A8:** BAD. "Rubber stamping" = approving without actually reviewing. This is an "unstaffed gate" — the human checkpoint exists on paper but no one actually checks. The exam tests whether you recognize this as a problem.

**A9:** NO. Legal content (contracts) always needs review by legal/compliance professionals. Claude can flag differences as a first pass, but a lawyer must verify the legal implications. Claude can fabricate legal analysis that sounds correct.

**A10:** RED tier (highest sensitivity). Social Security numbers must NEVER be uploaded to AI. If the task doesn't need SSNs (which is almost always), remove them completely before proceeding.
